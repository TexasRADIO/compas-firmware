# Compás research 03 · Mexico City sonideros, electronic cumbia and electro corridos

5 November 2026. Private research for the Sonidero screen and future rhythm studies. This extends [the first dossier](RESEARCH.md) and [Scenes and Machines](SCENES-AND-MACHINES.md).

**Evidence grades** · **Record** means a dated object, release, official text, credit or event programme. **Account** means first-person testimony or observed fieldwork, with the witness and scope retained. **Design** means a Compás musical proposal. A machine manual can identify a model and its capabilities. A production credit can establish that someone programmed a record while leaving the hardware unknown. A later rig can establish a touring practice while leaving earlier sessions open.

## 1 · Mexico City sonideros

**Origins and geography.** The city’s safeguarding dossier places the emergence of the culture in the late 1950s in Tepito, Peñón de los Baños, San Juan de Aragón and Tacubaya. Early parties could be held in the courtyards of *vecindades*, with record playing offered as a service. The Fonoteca Nacional names Guadalupe Reyes Salazar, La Socia, among the pioneering women who drew crowds in 1960s Tepito and whom Ramón Rojo helped at presentations. Iztapalapa appears in Manuel Perea’s retrospective account of early sound activity and should be treated as an oral-history lead. Nezahualcóyotl is a separate municipality in Estado de México. Mirjam Wirz documents a dance at Club Gangster’s Siglo XX there in 2011, and El Proyecto Sonidero ran a workshop on sonidero graphics and painted walls there in 2013. [CDMX decree, pp. 5–7](https://data.consejeria.cdmx.gob.mx/portal_old/uploads/gacetas/e00f549e7983a49c31510dd87898832e.pdf#page=5) · [Fonoteca Nacional on La Socia](https://www.gob.mx/cultura/prensa/la-socia-la-primera-sonidera-en-la-fonoteca-nacional) · [Perea interview](https://versusideasencontradas.wordpress.com/2013/06/11/el-sonidero-no-se-crea-ni-se-destruye-solo-se-transforma/) · [Wirz’s dated photographs and interview](https://norient.com/blog/sonidero-city/) · [Gráfica Sonidera](https://graficasonidera.wordpress.com/)

*Open.* The date of an individual system’s first paid dance and the date its permanent name appeared may differ. The late-1950s municipal chronology does not resolve earlier recollections or make Iztapalapa and Neza interchangeable with Tepito or Peñón.

**Record routes.** Ethnographer Alexandra Lippman interviewed sound operators and dealers in the Peñón and central city network, including Porky and José Ramírez Ortega, Morelos. They describe buying records in Colombia and circulating them through Mexican collectors and dances. Morelos’s family account at Casa del Lago recalls a land journey in the 1970s. Analog Africa’s compilation research identifies the Perea and Ortega networks and reports informal LP compilations in Tepito stalls from the early 1980s. Wirz photographed a Tepito record stall in 2011. Together these sources establish several connected routes, including collection trips and market recirculation. [Lippman’s fieldwork, pp. 207–209](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/25729861.2018.1497273) · [Casa del Lago event account via Fundación UNAM](https://www.fundacionunam.org.mx/pumarte/sonideros-el-sonido-de-todos-los-barrios/) · [Analog Africa’s *Super Disco Pirata* notes](https://analogafrica.bandcamp.com/album/super-disco-pirata-de-tepito-para-el-mundo-1965-1980-analog-africa-nr-39) · [Wirz](https://norient.com/blog/sonidero-city/)

*Open.* The first Colombian cumbia record imported into CDMX and the date of its first sonidero play require a dated copy, ledger or broadcast. The compilation’s 1965–80 span concerns the recorded repertoire. Its market account gives a different date for the LP copies’ circulation.

**Saludo and signal path.** Lippman watched Sonido La Conga at Salón Lazaros in Los Angeles. People offered signs and paper slips, or sent names over Messenger. The operator dipped the music at the mixer and spoke names of people and places over the record. In accounts from the Mexican scene the request can travel back to someone absent when the dance recording goes onto cassette or CD. The greeting starts with an audience contribution, and the performer chooses when and how to make space for it. Perea and Rojo recall an earlier stage without routine spoken dedications. The Fonoteca testimony and Wirz’s account of María Luz Perea, La Morena, make room for women’s work as selectors, record dealers, broadcasters and MCs. [Lippman’s fieldwork, pp. 209–214](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/25729861.2018.1497273) · [Early microphone accounts](https://versusideasencontradas.wordpress.com/2013/06/11/el-sonidero-no-se-crea-ni-se-destruye-solo-se-transforma/) · [Fonoteca](https://www.gob.mx/cultura/prensa/la-socia-la-primera-sonidera-en-la-fonoteca-nacional) · [Wirz](https://norient.com/blog/sonidero-city/)

*Open.* The evidence establishes ducking by hand, speech and echo as audible practices. It does not establish a fixed subdivision, decay, or standard reverb or echo unit.

**Carteles, walls and cabinets.** The 2013 Centro Cultural de España exhibition *Gráfica Sonidera* collected material from the 1970s onward. Co-producer Mariana Delgado names José Luis Lugo’s Publicidades Panther workshop, Luis E. Dueñas “Aníbal,” Canuto, Jaime Ruelas and Publicidades Lupano. Aníbal demonstrates cut-by-hand artwork in the project’s documentary. Panther supplied printing and Lupano the wall painters. The curators distinguish the local tropical print tradition from the futuristic electronic and Hi-NRG graphics represented by Ruelas and Polymarchs. Their archives document graphics on trucks, stage banners, windows, clothing and walls. [Gráfica Sonidera exhibition archive](https://graficasonidera.wordpress.com/) · [Delgado’s workshop account](https://graficasonidera.wordpress.com/portfolio/publicidades-panther-y-publicidades-anibal/) · [Ruelas and Lugo’s 2013 oral history](https://vimeo.com/86564149)

A UNAM thesis studied 16 posters and 58 calling cards from Joaquín González’s collection. The 1970s examples use inexpensive warm paper, vertical hierarchy and a prominent headliner. The thesis describes offset printing and localized ink mixing, such as yellow and blue making a green transition, with some screenprinting. It also shows a two-tone or dark blue La Conga card, a red-purple 1992–2001 anniversary piece and Ruelas’s Polymarchs lettering diagram with a stepped extrusion. The Sonido Dinamita poster photographed in the collection foregrounds date, fair, patronal day and sound name with a gramophone illustration. Its printed 26 June is legible, while the photograph date and the production date of the object are separate. The 2009 El Proyecto Sonidero archive indexes three dated Peñón patronal-fair flyers. [UNAM thesis, graphic communication chapter, figs. 71–75 and logo diagrams](https://tesiunamdocumentos.dgb.unam.mx/ptd2013/junio/0696536/0696536_A1.pdf) · [2009 Peñón flyers](https://elproyectosonidero.wordpress.com/tag/tepito/)

The event photographs anchor the screen’s physical motifs. Wirz’s dated views include the Sonido Amistad Caracas rig in Santiago de Iztacalco in 2010, Sonorámico at Campestre Aragón in 2011, La Conga setting up in Aragón in 2011 and a fan-jacket image with Sonido Super Dengue and Polymarchs in Pantitlán in 2014. Rojo and Linares describe carpenter-built *roperos*, the large speaker cabinets that preceded bigger rigs. The stacked enclosures and separate horn are credible design references. [Wirz’s field photographs](https://norient.com/blog/sonidero-city/) · [Rojo and Linares on cabinets](https://elrelojsemanal.com/nunca-pense-que-los-sonideros-serian-patrimonio-cultural-en-cdmx-ramon-rojo-de-la-changa/)

*Open.* Vivid flat colours, mixed inks and drawn depth are documented. Photographs and scans do not prove fluorescent ink chemistry, an exact palette, synchronized chase-light circuitry or a single period typeface. Uniform cabinet branding is also unverified. A label on a specific photographed cabinet should be credited to that rig. Jorge Luis González Balleza’s government-documented project adds contemporary testimony and visual documentation. It should remain a separate source from the 2013 exhibition and from Monterrey-area material. [González Balleza’s project](https://sistemacreacion.cultura.gob.mx/prensa/detalle/78030)

**Heritage status.** The official CDMX gazette no. 1208 published the city’s intangible cultural heritage declaration on 6 October 2023, pp. 5–7, with the decree providing for entry into force the following day. Participants in Musas Sonideras describe the public recognition ceremony on 8 October. The official publication date stays 6 October. The declaration invokes UNESCO’s convention as a framework. As checked 5 November 2026, UNESCO’s published Mexico inscriptions list contains no Cultura Sonidera entry. [Official gazette](https://data.consejeria.cdmx.gob.mx/portal_old/uploads/gacetas/e00f549e7983a49c31510dd87898832e.pdf#page=5) · [CDMX cultural inventory](https://repospatrimonio.cultura.cdmx.gob.mx/declaratorias-de-patrimonio-cdmx/cultura-sonidera/) · [Musas Sonideras participants on the ceremony](https://www.sinembargo.mx/4419205/las-mujeres-levantan-la-voz-entre-los-sonideros-que-la-sororidad-se-haga-costumbre/) · [UNESCO’s Mexico list](https://ich.unesco.org/es/estado/mexico-MX?info=elementos-en-las-listas)

*Open.* A proposal or future UNESCO nomination is distinct from an inscription. This is a dated check of the public register.

**Playback and electronic production.** Rojo recalls a 25 W valve Radson amplifier and a 12-inch Philips speaker for his early record-selling setup. Francisco González describes a domestic console at the beginning of Sonido Pancho. CCEMx’s 2018 programme adds a later Garrard turntable and Radson equipment and horns in its account of Pancho’s early upgrades. Lippman names the Technics 1200 family as a comparison for speed selection, without tying that deck to a particular named operator in the historical case. In a reproduced first-person account Marco Antonio Cedillo of Sonido Imperial describes modifying a Garrard 88 for greater speed range. At later dances recording, microphone and mixer can coexist with laptops, controller sets and streams. The relevant documented example is Amantes del Futuro’s Ableton Live and Akai APC set, with percussion clips and live microphone delay. A controller’s name does not identify the sound generator. [Rojo interview](https://biblialogos.blogspot.com/2009/11/los-inicios-del-movimiento-sonidero.html) · [Pancho family interview](https://tierraadentro.fondodeculturaeconomica.com/de-fiesta-callejera-historia-de-los-sonideros-de-tepito/) · [CCEMx on Pancho’s setup](https://ccemx.aecid.es/-/tropikillers-sonido-cubaney-sonido-pancho-de-tepito) · [Lippman, playback section](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/25729861.2018.1497273) · [Cedillo account and attribution caveats](https://periferiarecords.wordpress.com/2020/12/27/quien-invento-la-cumbia-rebajada/) · [Parque Explora live setup](https://www.parqueexplora.org/programate/orbitas-sonoras-amantes-del-futuro)

*Open.* No dated model-specific CDJ attribution, named period echo box or original TR-727, TR-808 or R-8 track credit was established for La Changa, La Conga or the five reviewed sonidos. A 2024 club video or current rider could establish a later live device when the panel and connection are legible. It would not identify an earlier studio recording’s source.

## 2 · Monterrey and rebajada

**The scene.** Darío Blanco Arboleda’s 2005 fieldwork documents Colombian Caribbean recordings and their local adoption in Monterrey. Colonia Independencia, the *colombias* or *kolombias* and the crossings between the central city and the hill neighbourhoods are part of the documented social geography. In first-person reporting from 2021, Dueñez recalls buying or receiving records through routes that included Mexico City and the Texas migrant network. Hairstyles, loose clothing and ways of dancing became signs of youth belonging, with local style and unequal access to public space central to the account. *Ya no estoy aquí* is a later dramatization and a route into the history. [Blanco Arboleda’s fieldwork](https://paperity.org/p/190289337/transculturalidad-y-procesos-identificatorios-la-musica-caribena-colombiana-en-monterrey) · [2021 reporting and interviews](https://elpais.com/mexico/2021-03-14/cumbia-un-hogar-para-los-sin-nombre-de-monterrey.html)

*Open.* A single group or movie costume cannot stand for the entire Monterrey scene. Map dates to the named collectors, events and neighbourhoods.

**The origin story and the record.** Gabriel Dueñez describes a malfunction and dancers staying with the slower playback. Accounts disagree about the fault, the equipment and the date. A 2021 interview report describes a worn motor, Analog Africa tells a short-circuit version and Debit dates the cassette practice she revisited to the 1990s. The label’s notes separately describe Cedillo’s deliberate pitch modification in CDMX. Gaby Dueñez and collaborators describe the continuing family archive in 2023. *Saturno 2000* is a 2022 compilation with a repertoire span of 1962–83 and modern pitch-control credit. Its track dates are not dates for the edited playback. Debit’s *Desaceleradas* appeared on 7 November 2025 and documents use of original Dueñez cassette sources, an ARP 2600, accordion and granular treatment. This is strong evidence for a named contemporary project, separate from the historical playback rig. [Dueñez interview and reporting](https://elpais.com/mexico/2021-03-14/cumbia-un-hogar-para-los-sin-nombre-de-monterrey.html) · [Analog Africa notes and credits](https://analogafrica.bandcamp.com/album/saturno-2000-la-rebajada-de-los-sonideros-1962-1983-analog-africa-nr-34) · [Reissue notes on pitch credit and edition limits](https://www.theartsdesk.com/new-music/music-reissues-weekly-saturno-2000-la-rebajada-de-los-sonideros-1962-1983) · [Dueñez family archive](https://www.jornada.com.mx/2023/07/01/espectaculos/a05n1esp) · [Debit’s project account](https://debit.media/performances/desaceleradas/)

*Open.* The initiating event and exact speed of the first playback remain unverified. The tapes and accounts establish sustained practice. Matching identified originals to original cassettes would allow a reproducible measurement of actual ratios.

**Ratios.** Daniel Rincón, a CDMX-based artist, describes playing 45 rpm records at 33 with a small positive pitch adjustment. If his “+5” means five percent on the pitch control, the ratio is about **0.78×**, or **−4.3 semitones**. The standard 33⅓/45 switch alone yields **0.741×**, or **−5.2 semitones**. Bandcamp’s interviews around *Saturno 2000* describe a **33-to-20** Sonido Imperial example, approximately **0.60×**, or **−8.8 semitones**. Another account mentions 25 rpm on a 33⅓ rpm record, equivalent to **0.75×** and **−5.0 semitones**. These ratios use pitch displacement = 12 × log₂(playback-rate ratio). They are mechanism examples, with an attributed lower extreme. [Rincón interview](https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/dubwise-cumbia-daniel-rincon-nap/) · [Bandcamp reporting with Joyce Musicolor and project participants](https://dailytest.bandcamp.com/features/saturno-2000-la-rebajada-de-los-sonideros-feature) · [Naiz account](https://www.naiz.eus/eu/gaiak/noticia/20220710/saturno-2000-la-rebajada-de-los-sonideros)

*Open.* A statistical typical speed for Monterrey cannot be inferred from these examples. Rincón describes his practice and the lower setting is reported for Cedillo’s CDMX sound.

**Celso Piña’s crossover.** *Barrio Bravo* was presented by Piña in July 2001. “Cumbia sobre el río (Suena)” opens the album and brings Piña’s accordion-led material together with Toy Hernández (Toy Selectah), Pato Machete of Control Machete and Blanquito Man of King Changó. Producer Julián Villarreal describes giving Toy the instrumental and asking for Pato and Blanquito. Toy identifies it as one 2001 production he was asked to do for the project and explains his aim of connecting the local sound to reggae and hip-hop sound-system culture. The launch report and the two producer accounts make this a dated crossover credit. [Contemporary launch coverage with Piña](https://www.jornada.com.mx/2001/07/06/31an1esp.html) · [Villarreal interview](https://www.milenio.com/espectaculos/musica/julian-villarreal-entrevista-productor-de-album-de-celso-pina) · [Toy’s lecture and record example](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/toy-selectah-toy-story/)

*Open.* Toy’s account of MPC use and earlier Sound Designer work concerns his wider practice and early Control Machete. The *Barrio Bravo* evidence reviewed does not identify the model of the box Villarreal recalls supplying or establish a machine for “Cumbia sobre el río.”

## 3 · Electronic cumbia scene map

The notes identify pulse or instrument roles where the source supports them. A recorded master, tempo analysis and a bar grid are still required for scene-wide BPM ranges or track-level kick and scraper placement. A suggested Compás tempo in §5 is a design decision.

**Kumbia Kings, Texas–Mexico, 1999–2000s.** The 1999 EMI Latin/Capitol catalogue of *Amor, Familia y Respeto* credits A. B. Quintanilla III with production and Cruz Martínez with programming and co-arrangement across tracks. Credits for “Fuiste Mala” name Ricardo Muñoz and guitar from Chris Pérez, while “Reggae Kumbia” places Vico C in a Caribbean and rap crossover. Accordion, bass-led cumbia, live playing, synth parts and programmed roles can coexist within this repertoire. Keep a drummer or percussion lane independent of the electronics when designing an accompaniment. [Capitol catalogue and track credits](https://www.qobuz.com/at-de/album/amor-familia-y-respeto-ab-quintanilla-iii-kumbia-all-starz/0724349918955) · [Alternate catalogue, 23 March 1999](https://www.prestomusic.com/jazz/products/9449865--amor-familia-y-respeto)

*Open.* Programming is documented at track-credit level. These credits do not identify a drum machine, source samples, software or a kick-and-scraper template shared by the repertoire.

**Nortec, Tijuana, from 1999.** Pepe Mogt (Fussible) circulated banda and norteño material among Ramón Amezcua (Bostich), Roberto Mendoza (Panóptica) and colleagues. The producers date their early collective releases to 1999. Amezcua identifies specific tools on “Polaris” in a 2010 interview. Banda percussion went through a vocoder and Waldorf D-Pole filtering, the kick came from a Roland SH-09, he edited the tuba in ACID and assembled the track in Cubase. Syncopated banda snare and tuba are audible structural elements in the contemporary coverage. In the same interview, Mogt describes a modified TR-909 kick as part of his wider method and names later live instruments including TR-606, TB-303 and Jomox MBase01. In a 2023 retrospective, the producers associate much of the 2008 *Tijuana Sound Machine* album with Akai MPC work and describe Pro Tools and Ableton Live postproduction. This is an early, well-documented regional-Mexican/electronic precedent. [Production interview and track workflow](https://nitro-press.com/entrevistanortec/) · [2001 coverage with Amezcua](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-feb-10-ca-23431-story.html) · [Artists’ 2008 album listing](https://norteccollective.bandcamp.com/album/bostich-fussible-tijuana-sound-machine) · [Album retrospective](https://24-horas.mx/vida/nortec-bostich-fussible-entre-la-violencia-y-la-fiesta/)

*Open.* A later TR-909 account cannot replace Amezcua’s SH-09 credit for “Polaris.” The exact MPC model for *Tijuana Sound Machine* and track-by-track allocation remain open. Nortec’s named precedent does not establish direct influence on all subsequent Mexican fusions or a common BPM.

**Cumbia villera, Buenos Aires suburbs, late 1990s–2000s.** Pablo Lescano recalls using Roland XP-10 and later Korg N364 keyboards. He also describes the circulation of the sampled güiro sound. Damas Gratis exemplifies the performance logic of keyboard lead and a repeated scraper over dance bass and percussion, within a suburban and working-class network. The sampled scraper is its own voice and can be articulated without following each kick. [Lescano on keyboards](https://www.rionegro.com.ar/pablo-lescano-me-converti-en-un-artista-ortodoxo-956531/) · [Lescano on the güiro](https://wwwgcp.clarin.com/espectaculos/pablo-lescano-careta_0_H1xNqGhRaYe.html) · [UNLP study](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/149120/Documento_completo.pdf?sequence=1)

*Open.* Keyboard model accounts are stronger than evidence for a named early sampler, BPM band or universal fixed rhythm. Keep villera separate from the later ZZK production network.

**Zizek/ZZK, Buenos Aires and Mexico City, from 2006.** Interviews used in scene research trace Buenos Aires’s Zizek party series from 2006 and the label as a later home for Chancha Vía Circuito, Fauna, El Remolón, King Coya and Frikstailers. ZZK issued *ZZK Sound Vol. 1* in 2008 and used the term “digital cumbia.” Live and studio work explores chopped percussion and folk records, synth bass, dub and other club rhythms. Frikstailers’ 2013 MUTEK Mexico presentation at Foro La Faena and the label’s identification of the duo as Mexico City-based on *Crop Circles* document a concrete CDMX link. Research with Zizek participants records Toy Selectah as an earlier Mexican reference among several independent antecedents. [Fieldwork with organisers](https://revistas.usal.es/cuatro/index.php/1130-2887/article/download/alh20187887104/18755) · [ZZK compilation](https://zzkrecords.bandcamp.com/album/zzk-sound-vol-1) · [ZZK on *Crop Circles*](https://zzkrecords.com/album/crop-circles) · [MUTEK Mexico event coverage](https://filtermexico.com/2013/09/18/mutek-mx-presenta-frikstailers/)

*Open.* A CDMX booking and residency do not prove the label began there. The varied catalogue does not establish one pulse, a common kick grid or a hardware standard.

**Tribal guarachero, Mexico City, Monterrey and Matehuala, late 2000s–early 2010s.** Early CDMX tribal precedes the Monterrey breakout. Erick Rincón, Sergio Zavala (Sheeqo Beat) and DJ Otto formed 3BallMTY around 2009. Their early internet releases circulated on market CDs. Youth dance crews and *botas picudas* had their own venues and contests in Matehuala, San Luis Potosí. Rincón’s accounts link his approach to Colombian cumbia heard in Monterrey, electronics and the tropical/tribal vocabulary. The 2013 3BallMTY interview confirms FL Studio as the group’s working DAW, and DJ Otto gave **132 BPM** as a representative pulse in 2012. “Pre-Hispanic” appears in the scene’s descriptions of timbre and influence. It does not date or identify the audio sources in a track. [3BallMTY on circulation](https://www.informador.mx/Entretenimiento/El-colectivo-3BallMTY-se-presenta-hoy-en-Calle-2-20120218-0129.html) · [FL Studio interview](https://remezcla.com/music/interview-3ballmty-from-under-the-overground/) · [DJ Otto’s tempo account](https://peopleenespanol.com/article/3ballmty-un-proyecto-global-muy-bailable/) · [Matehuala reporting with Rincón](https://www.vice.com/es/article/botas-picudas-matehuala-tribal-erick-rincon/) · [Dance and dress research](https://doi.org/10.4000/transatlantica.7596)

*Open.* A representative tempo is not a range, and no authenticated sample pack or single early drum machine was established. Use original drum and scrape figures for a prototype. Specific kick and scraper patterns should be measured on a pinned recording before making a historical claim.

**CDMX electronic sonidera.** Ima Felini recalls an early electronic cumbia made with Diego Ceballos (Metrika) on a Roland Groovebox 303 and dates his more sustained Amantes del Futuro project to 2002. The recollection says roughly 1995 or 1996. Roland dates the MC-303 to 1996, so the chronology fits 1996 or later if the model is remembered correctly. Felini distinguishes Mexico City sonidera’s percussion, piano and horns from Poblana cumbia’s more overt synth palette. ## 5 · Design implications

1. **Prototype a coupled rebajada control.** Put normal speed at 1.00× and audition a 0.75× target, a 0.78× softer setting and a 0.60× lower edge as an attributed extreme. Keep it distinct from the BPM knob and from external clock behaviour. Treat the proposed knob range as a product decision, with 0.60× pending stage and audio checks. 2. **Make room for a live saludo.** An optional short, player-authored message in a display cue would reflect names and dedications being requested from the floor. **Tribal 132** is a two-bar 4/4 accompaniment for a live lead or singer.
The default sound uses Compás synthesis. In each two-beat group the kick
lands on steps 0 and 4, the guacharaca accents steps 0 and 6, and the low
drum answers at 3 and 6. The bass picks up at 2 and 6. The snare falls on
beats two and four. Lift adds the offbeat shaker and short drum pickups.
Break leaves the kick, guacharaca and low drum under the performer.
The fill adds a low-drum figure at the end of the second bar. These
placements and the duple grid are original design choices. The study
contains no sampled artist audio and plays no melody from a recording.
