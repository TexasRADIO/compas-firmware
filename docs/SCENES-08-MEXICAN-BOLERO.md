# Mexican bolero · dated scores and early recordings

## Júrame, María Grever

Grever's song belongs to the Mexican bolero repertory in the Archivo General
de la Nación's account. The New York Schirmer print calls it *Spanish Tango*,
gives it a 2/4 meter and prints voice and piano with an English version by
Frederick H. Martens. The first sheet carries the 1926 U.S. notice and a
separate 1928 notice for Mexico. The three-page scan is IMSLP 675258,
plate 37700. Júrame was recorded by José Mojica with orchestra. UCLA dates
the performance associated with its Victor Collectors Issue to September
22, 1927. The HMV discography lists original take BVE-40023-3 for
DA 1193 and also cross-references that Collectors Issue. The two
records separate the session matrix from the reissue matrix.
[Archivo General de la Nación exhibition](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/exponentes-de-la-m%C3%BAsica-mexicana-en-la-primera-mitad-del-siglo-xx/uQXhyDbiNm1DiQ)
· [Schirmer score](https://petruccimusiclibrary.ca/files/imglnks/caimg/d/db/IMSLP675258-PMLP1083489-Grever.pdf)
· [UCLA Victor Collectors Issue](https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/j%C3%BArame-25)
· [HMV discography, DA 1193](https://adp-assets.library.ucsb.edu/HMVCelebritySeriesDiscography.pdf)

The full **Júrame** rehearsal plays the five-bar piano introduction,
the first stanza and its refrain through the D.C. on page 3. It
returns to the beginning for stanza two and ends after the second
refrain. The print gives a second stanza and a D.C. without a Fine.
Ending after one return is a rehearsal decision. The result is 90
bars in performance, from 45 written bars.

The star and bracket in written measure 20 mark three vocal notes to
omit in the second stanza. The guide and the MIDI leave an
18-tick gap for those three sixteenths, with the accompaniment still
running. The MIDI keeps the paired notes on the vocal staff, including
the tied continuation into measure 41. The FM-1's single-voice guide
takes the upper pitch at paired attacks. The final tied C carries
into the last bar before the printed rests.

The piano cue selects lower-staff attacks through the song and adds
the top line of the five-bar piano introduction. The full piano
texture remains to be transcribed. The rolled opening intervals sound
together. The rehearsal uses a fixed editorial quarter-note tempo
of 72, with the printed rubato, accelerando and fermatas realized at
their nominal note lengths.

## Yucatán and Nuevo León

Luis Pérez Sabido places *Madrigal* in 1918 at Hacienda Cacao, with music
by Enrique Galaz Chacón and words by Carlos R. Menéndez González. The
2018 centenary notice reports a Ponce–Galaz premiere and identifies
the *Álbum de canciones yucatecas* as a score route. Its body has a
century typo. The 2012 anthology index places the song at p. 158 and
credits Pedro Carlos Herrera López with score preparation and
adaptation. The Baqueiro Fóster collection is a route to the earlier
witness. The 1918 date is a composition claim.
[Pérez Sabido's account](https://sipse.com/novedades-yucatan/columna-luis-perez-sabido-musica-fuera-397208.html)
· [2018 centenary](https://www.cultura.gob.mx/noticias/musica/56215-se-celebran-100-anos-de-%E2%80%9Cmadrigal%E2%80%9D-primer-bolero-mexicano.html)
· [2012 anthology](https://es.scribd.com/document/373419306/Boleros-Yucatecos-pdf)
· [Fonoteca catalogue of holdings](https://fonotecanacional.cultura.gob.mx/servicios/consulta-del-acervo)

For Armando Villarreal Lozano's *Morena mía*, the image reproduced
from Pablo Dueñas shows the heading *Canción colombiana*, a piano
opening and the start of the sung line. Its caption dates publication
to 1921. The crop ends partway through the vocal system and excludes
the imprint. The published title and subtitle can be compared with the
same genre tag on UCLA's copy of the 1926 Briceño–Añez side, Victor
78799-B, matrix BVE29760-6. Keep that dated take separate from the
1924 recording reported by Dueñas.
[Sáenz Pérez, fig. 5, digitized p. 23](https://repository.udistrital.edu.co/bitstreams/f727b9e7-b21b-4c8a-b479-633d4d8846f2/download#page=23)
· [CID catalogue for Dueñas's book](https://cid-albertobeltran.cultura.gob.mx/catalogo-biblioteca/bolero-historia-documental-del-bolero-mexicano-pablo-duenas-h/)
· [UCLA, Victor 78799-B](https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/morena-m%C3%ADa-14)

The dated 1927 Wagner y Levien edition of *Nunca* is a different
kind of lead. Madrid's Real Academia gives the three-page
voice-and-piano print a specific shelfmark, M-6331. Its score image
is unavailable in the catalogue. The Fonoteca's Guty Cárdenas
microsite distinguishes Guty's clave from Ernesto Paredes's earlier
bolero to the same Ricardo López Méndez words.
[Real Academia, M-6331](https://www.academiacolecciones.com/musica/inventario.php?id=M-6331)

## The Cuban sources

Sáenz Pérez reproduces both facing pages of *Tristezas* from Helio
Orovio's 1995 *El bolero latino*. They show a printed piano
introduction, sung notation and the closing double bar. The ledger
separates this witness from the *resDanza* single-page image, the
Perucho Mejía melodic guide and the early recordings. A named early
print or the rights history of the reproduced piano arrangement
remains to be established. Díaz-Ayala dates the Trío Matamoros
*Lágrimas negras*, matrix XVE 67176, to February 24, 1931. This
recording, the 1932 Brito edition and later performances have separate
entries in the [repertoire ledger](REPERTOIRE.md).
