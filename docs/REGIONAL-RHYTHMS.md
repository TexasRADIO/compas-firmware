# Regional electronic cumbia bank

6 November 2026 · Offline Compás candidate

The factory set includes the first six regional accompaniments in slots
11–16. The **Regional Cumbia** edition gathers those six and four newer studies
in a ten-slot set that starts on CDMX Sonidera. These are original Compás
arrangements. The evidence ledger below identifies the documented musical roles.
Exact steps, bass lines, fills and most starting tempos are Compás choices.

Open **Compas-Regional-Cumbia.html**, enable audio, raise MASTER and press
PLAY. Turn PRESETS on Band to cue another study at the phrase. The native
Mac emulator handover opens the same set with its own virtual saves.
Use the [regional audition set](../repertoire/regional-audition-set.json)
with **Open set or show** to load this running order into the main browser.
All ten can also be selected individually in Set Builder and in the
[complete groove bank](GROOVE-PRESET-BANK.md) under REC → PRESETS.
SEQ opens the separate [percussion part bank](AFRO-CUBAN-727-BANK.md).

Press PLAY, cue Chorus for Lift or Break for the reduced backing, and use ARP
for a fill. The Bass page's Ensemble/Acid choice is available for all ten;
its synthetic sound is a Compás performance option. Bass has not been replaced
by a 727 hit. CDMX, Villera and Chile default to Hybrid for conga, with Villera
also routing high timbale. Puebla uses Hybrid low timbale and Buenos Aires
Digital uses Hybrid maracas for the shaker. Scraper, bass and synth cues keep
their own engines. Other slots start on synthesis. The four optional percussion layers
are live Compás overlays, not independently sourced regional patterns.

## Mexican family development addition

**Cumbia Sureste** is retained as a source-labelled arrangement study, not a
claim. A Chiapas academic thesis and ENES Mérida material identify the
regional ensemble roles: drum kit and tumbadoras, sax/brass lead space, keys
or electric-guitar colour and a flexible bass function. Compás turns those
roles into original two-bar 4/4 timing at 108 BPM; it reproduces no recording,
lead line, chart or source bass line. The sax/horn role is intentionally left
for a performer. It still needs review with a southeast player before it can
be described as locally reviewed. [Source ledger and limits](MEXICAN-SONIDERO-VARIANTS.md).

## Evidence and adaptation ledger

**REG-CDMX — Mexico City.** The Amantes del Futuro venue programme explicitly
names congas, bells, güiro/guacharaca, voice fragments and live greetings with
microphone delay. The dossier's Felini interviews distinguish the sonidera
piano/horn vocabulary from Poblana synth emphasis. This supports separate
percussion roles and room for a performer. It does not supply a step grid.
The new slot has no sampled speech, horn, piano or microphone delay.
[Venue's live-rig description](https://www.parqueexplora.org/programate/orbitas-sonoras-amantes-del-futuro)
· [Local research](SCENES-03-CDMX-AND-ELECTRO.md#3--electronic-cumbia-scene-map)

**REG-MTY — Monterrey crossover.** Toy Selectah's account and the producer
interviews in dossier 03 connect Celso Piña's *Barrio Bravo* project with
reggae and hip-hop sound-system production. The study combines a cumbia
scraper with bass space, rim and offbeat chord cues as an original arrangement.
It does not play “Cumbia sobre el río.” No machine model is assigned to that
track. A pluck is a synthetic chord cue, not a sampled accordion.
[Toy Selectah's first-person lecture](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/toy-selectah-toy-story/)
· [Local source chain](SCENES-03-CDMX-AND-ELECTRO.md#2--monterrey-and-rebajada)

**Tribal — CDMX/Monterrey/Matehuala.** The existing Tribal 132 study is now a
factory slot. DJ Otto's 132 BPM account is a participant benchmark; its duple
step grid remains a Compás composition. The scene's wider rhythmic variety
is not represented by one loop.
[Existing ledger and participant references](SCENES-03-CDMX-AND-ELECTRO.md#6--playable-study--tribal-132)

**REG-VILLERA — Buenos Aires suburbs.** Pablo Lescano identifies the XP-10
and N364 and discusses physical keyboards. His separate scraper account and
the local research establish a keyboard-led practice distinct from the later
Zizek/ZZK circuit. The new bass, scraper and timbal phrases are authored; the
machine reproduces neither those keyboard patches nor a Damas Gratis song.
[Lescano/Télam interview](https://www.rionegro.com.ar/pablo-lescano-me-converti-en-un-artista-ortodoxo-956531/)
· [Local research and scraper source](SCENES-AND-MACHINES.md#cumbias-dance-floors)

**REG-LIMA — later digital cumbia.** Dengue Dengue Dengue's Tokyo 2014 lecture
describes programming, 808 sounds, sampling and recreating rhythms electronically.
That is evidence for a method and palette, not an exact record pattern. The
study uses original synthesized sounds and a broken beat. It is not labelled
as a traditional chicha ensemble, a Los Mirlos guitar arrangement or a faithful
808 emulation. A different lead can be played over it.
[Artist lecture and transcript](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/dengue-dengue-dengue-tokyo-2014/)
· [Local regional distinctions](SCENES-AND-MACHINES.md#cumbias-dance-floors)

**REG-CHILE — electronic crossover.** The artist reissue of *Gonzalo Martínez y
sus Congas Pensantes* lists electronic instruments and production tools at
album level, including Drumulator, Linn 9000, Simmons SDS 7 and Akai S3000.
The Biblioteca Nacional de Chile catalogue holds a 1997 notice. The reissue's
2021 date is not the original album date. These sources support exploring
cumbia with programmed percussion and synths; the four-quarter kick and
112 BPM setting are Compás choices, not transcribed album evidence.
[Artist reissue credits](https://gonzalomartinez.bandcamp.com/album/gonzalo-martinez-y-sus-congas-pensantes)
· [1997 catalogue notice](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-82969.html)

**REG-PUEBLA — Puebla synth cumbia.** Ima Felini singles out percussion,
synthesizers and voice. Puebla's municipal heritage journal describes bands
using keyboards to emulate the sonidero sound, and Orihuela MSS recalls
local sequencers and computer production. Compás supplies a short root/fifth
synth cue, timbal pickups and space for a live lead. The groove does not
assign a machine model or claim a single Puebla rhythm.
[Felini interview](https://www.semana.com/cultura/articulo/el-amor-cumbiero-atemporal-de-amantes-del-futuro-vemos-a-colombia-como-la-cumbia-madre/202546/)
· [Puebla municipal journal](https://centrohistorico.pueblacapital.gob.mx/media/com_dpattachments/attachments/com_content.article/Revista-Cuetlaxcoapan-31.pdf)
· [Orihuela MSS interview](https://globalbassstudies.substack.com/p/entrevista-a-orihuela-mss)

**REG-WEPA — San Luis Potosí.** Wepa needs a narrower claim than a
digital-cut genre label. A Colegio de México study records the Potosí use as
a cumbia-like sound-world marked by deep voices announcing names, barrios and
greetings; it is close to, but not synonymous with, *sonidero*. Felini treats
it as a separate approach and names San Luis Potosí in his Monterrey
interview. Compás makes no sampled voice claim: the alternating short synth
cuts, grid, tempo and bass are authored, and the Break leaves a player-created
gap for the live call.
[Felini on wepa](https://www.semana.com/cultura/articulo/el-amor-cumbiero-atemporal-de-amantes-del-futuro-vemos-a-colombia-como-la-cumbia-madre/202546/)
· [Colegio de México study](https://linguisticamexicana-amla.colmex.mx/index.php/Linguistica_mexicana/article/download/449/431/2691)

**REG-TECNO-PERU — Peruvian tecnocumbia.** Rossy War recalls Tito Mauri
bringing in electronic tumbas, drums and synthesizers. Compás translates the
instrument roles into two tumba registers, electronic backbeat and bass.
Use it as a singer's backing. The interview supplies neither a step grid nor
a machine model. Lima Digital remains the later club-oriented study.
[Rossy War interview](https://m.peru21.pe/cultura/rossy-war-la-tecnocumbia-revitalizo-la-cumbia-entrevista-20220616231426/)
· [Peruvian scene research](SCENES-AND-MACHINES.md#cumbias-dance-floors)

**REG-BA-DIGITAL — Buenos Aires digital circuit.** The Zizek party and the
2008 ZZK compilation document a broad electronic cumbia practice. This
study uses an asymmetric pair of bars, percussive bass and gated-sounding
synth stabs in Lift. Villera retains its keyboard-led band arrangement.
Neither study reproduces a release or an artist preset.
[Original compilation](https://zzkrecords.bandcamp.com/album/zzk-sound-vol-1)
· [Villa Diamante interview](https://www.vice.com/es/article/productores-de-por-aca-villa-diamante/)
· [Local dossier](SCENES-03-CDMX-AND-ELECTRO.md#3--electronic-cumbia-scene-map)

## What remains distinct

[Rebajada SPEED](REBAJADA.md) changes tempo, pitch and tails together.
Monterrey Cumbia Dub is a separate composition. The contemporary electro
corrido crossover is documented in dossier 03 and remains outside this
cumbia set. The era-specific Johnny Canales tribute uses its own running order.

## Compatibility and review

The four new study IDs are appended after the existing Merengue Congas ID.
The factory order and show layout are preserved. Use the rebuilt machine to
play a set or show that references the new IDs. Previously saved shows reopen
with their own running order. The expansion adds constant pattern data and
uses the existing voices, conductor and 727 routing.

These are playable studies of regional approaches. Review with players from
each scene and passage-level comparisons remain open before making claims of
regional fidelity. See [the pattern source](../src/score.json) for the exact
programmed parts and their source IDs.
