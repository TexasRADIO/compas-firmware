# Compás research 02 · Scenes, machines and the evidence between them

5 November 2026. This extends [the first dossier](RESEARCH.md). A maker's
manual proves a capability. An artist interview documents the artist's
account. A dated credit, session log or demonstrable recording link is needed
to attribute a particular machine to a track. An electronic pad can trigger a
separate module or sampler. A slow record, a keyboard rhythm and a programmed
drum machine are different production methods.

## Cumbia's dance floors

**Colombian Caribbean.** The Ministry of Cultures and its community
safeguarding plan describe a family of local practices in the Bajo Magdalena
and surrounding regions. A coastal study may foreground the llamador, the
tambora and the answering alegre, with maraca or guache and room for millo or
gaita. These roles come from the documented ensemble. The existing Compás
coastal study keeps its synthesized approximations and no bass lane. In his
ministry profile, Pedro Beltrán's Cumbia Moderna de Soledad illustrates how a
named coastal ensemble could add bass, accordion and horns while retaining a
percussion conversation. Sources: [Ministry overview and safeguarding plan](https://mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Paginas/servicios-informacion/LRPCI/cumbia-tradicional-del-caribe-colombiano.aspx),
[Pedro Beltrán, Ministry volume](https://www.mincultura.gov.co/despacho/Documents/micasa/2025/MiCASa%20-%20Premio%20Nacional%20Vida%20y%20Obra%202023%20%28libro%20digital%29.pdf).

**Picó and champeta.** Cartagena and Barranquilla sound-system circuits share
a coast with cumbia and have their own record and dance history. In interview,
Bomba Estéreo's Simón Mejía describes picós playing African and Caribbean
records, and local musicians adapting them with keyboards and built-in
rhythm. Mixmag's field report identifies the Casio SK-5 as a sampler used in
champeta and guarapo and describes present-day MPC performance. This is
adjacent evidence for sampling and live triggering, with a named model in the
latter report. It leaves model and session evidence for early coastal cumbia
open. Sources: [Mejía interview](https://www.mondosonoro.com/entrevistas/bomba-estereo-entrevista/),
[Picó field reporting](https://mixmag.net/feature/pico-sound-systems-colombian-bass-culture-going-global-champeta-urbana-guarapo).

**Mexico City and Monterrey.** Sonideros built audiences through records,
announcements, dedications, neighbourhood dances, speakers and later recorded
mixes. Jorge Luis González Balleza's project documents the living Mexico City
scene. In Monterrey, scholarship drawing on Gabriel Dueñez and other scene
participants describes Colombian recordings, tapes, altered playback speeds,
Colonia Independencia and the youth culture around música colombiana.
Dueñez's account of an accidental speed drop is oral testimony. His daughter's
account helps establish family practice and a continuing archive. Pitch and
tempo move together when a record is slowed. That mechanism gives a potential
future rebajada control a different purpose from a slow preset tempo.
Sources: [González Balleza / Secretaría de Cultura](https://sistemacreacion.cultura.gob.mx/prensa/detalle/78030),
[Monterrey fieldwork](https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/alte/v15n30/2448-850X-alte-15-30-19.pdf),
[Gabriel Dueñez interview](https://www.vice.com/es/article/radio-alternativa-rebajado-el-podcast-con-la-cumbia-mas-fina/),
[Gaby Dueñez and archive collaborators](https://www.jornada.com.mx/2023/07/01/espectaculos/a05n1esp).

**Mexican electronic cumbia and quebradita.** Casimiro Zamudio describes
Mi Banda El Mexicano's electronic dance experiments from Mazatlán and their
relationship to wider musical influences. The interview establishes his
approach more clearly than a machine identification. Jorge Medina Martínez,
a musician and Roland México product specialist, describes R-8 use in Mexican
tropical music, including cumbia, and in keyboardist-led dance acts that
needed to recall and shape backing arrangements live. His examples describe
scene practice and product reception, with few session particulars. The
2020s Hanan Cumbia provides direct documentation of a Mexican maker's compact
kick, güiro, cowbell and conga workflow. Its graphics cite Peruvian chicha.
Together these motivate the existing electronic study, direct pads and
phrase-aware changes. The evidence still leaves individual Mi Banda El
Mexicano drum-machine assignments open. Sources: [Zamudio interview](https://www.vice.com/es/article/pa-que-lo-bailes-una-charla-con-casimiro-zamudio-de-mi-banda-el-mexicano/),
[Medina Martínez](https://articles.roland.com/the-legacy-of-the-r-8-human-rhythm-composer-in-mexico/),
[Hanan manufacturer manual](https://www.oficinadesonido.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Hanan-Cumbia.pdf).

**Peru.** Los Shapis and Chacalón belong to an Andean migrant and working-class
history in Lima, with electric guitars and huayno alongside tropical rhythms.
Jorge Rodríguez Granda's Los Mirlos interview gives a distinct account of
Amazonian psychedelic guitar cumbia. Jorge Olazo's scene study reports
Roland Octapad use from the late 1980s in chicha. Octapad is a MIDI controller
and the sound source requires separate evidence. Rossy War explicitly recalls
Tito Mauri using electronic tumbas, drums and synthesizers in Peruvian
tecnocumbia. Her interview names the sound categories but leaves the boxes
unidentified. Later Dengue Dengue Dengue describe live Maschine performance,
Ableton loops, programming and 808 sounds. That is strong first-person
evidence for a later digital approach, including software and samples.
Sources: [Los Shapis interview](https://peru21.pe/cultura/los-shapis-el-aguajal-fue-la-llave-para-entrar-a-lima/),
[Los Mirlos interview](https://soundsandcolours.com/articles/peru/interview-with-los-mirlos-jorge-rodriguez-granda-20661/),
[Lima scene research](https://revistas.cientifica.edu.pe/index.php/desdeelsur/article/download/1361/1082/6284),
[Roland on Octapad](https://articles.roland.com/redefining-rhythm-a-history-of-roland-drums/),
[Rossy War interview](https://m.peru21.pe/cultura/rossy-war-la-tecnocumbia-revitalizo-la-cumbia-entrevista-20220616231426/),
[Dengue Dengue Dengue lecture](https://www.redbullmusicacademy.com/lectures/dengue-dengue-dengue-tokyo-2014/),
[their live rig interview](https://cdm.link/electric-psychedelic-dengue-dengue-dengue-talk-peru-cumbia-playing-live-visuals/).

**Buenos Aires and the digital circuits.** Pablo Lescano recalls inexpensive
Roland XP-10 and later Korg N364 keyboards giving early cumbia villera groups
a shared sound, and identifies the sampled güiro as a shortcut to imitating
his instrumentation. These are named synth/workstation and sampling practices.
Cumbia villera's suburban and working-class scene is historically distinct from
the later Buenos Aires Zizek/ZZK party and production network, which exchanged
ideas with Lima and New York. This suggests separate future studies for live
keyboard-led villera and loop-based digital cumbia. Sources:
[Lescano on instruments](https://www.rionegro.com.ar/pablo-lescano-me-converti-en-un-artista-ortodoxo-956531/),
[Lescano on the sampled güiro](https://wwwgcp.clarin.com/espectaculos/pablo-lescano-careta_0_H1xNqGhRaYe.html),
[UNLP research](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/149120/Documento_completo.pdf?sequence=1),
[research with ZZK participants](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0716-27902016000200003&script=sci_arttext).

## Salsa and the Texas borderlands

**Salsa.** The Smithsonian traces Cuban son and rumba and Puerto Rican
contributions in the US. Willie Colón described salsa as a New York sound and
word in 1988. Percussionist Luis Bauzó Delgado's account connects the East
Harlem Music School and working bands, with player-led bongó and timbal.
Salsa romántica studio polish and electronic effects appear in the literature,
but sonic polish alone leaves the identity of the rhythm source open. The
research for this pass did not yield a primary named 727, 909 or R-8 session
credit for a 1980s salsa recording. The R-8's Mexican dance-band use is a
different context from the New York studio and dance circuits. Compás therefore
preserves the clave and shell/bell relationship across sections. Hybrid 727
adds muted/open conga and maraca while clave, cáscara and campana keep their
own jobs. Sources: [Smithsonian Salsa's Roots](https://latino.si.edu/exposiciones/puro-ritmo/qr/salsas-roots-case),
[Willie Colón interview](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1988-12-23-ca-697-story.html),
[percussionist account](https://maestravida.com/siembra30.com/LBDelgadoSiembra001.pdf),
[Mexico City salsa fieldwork](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0w37r5f8/qt0w37r5f8_noSplash_e3862c263e0ef3038afae1dbe8f24f80.pdf).

**Cuban song and conjunto.** The 1929 *Cuba musical* setting of
*La Má Teodora* sets calls against harmonized responses in 6/8. The
catalog keeps the disputed origin story, the later güiro arrangement
and the 1929 notation separate. Arsenio Rodríguez’s 1942 *Como traigo la
yuca*, better known as *Dile a Catalina*, is a different reference.
FIU identifies Victor 83948-B as a guaracha. Alejandro Fernández Bernal’s
notated example places one phrase against 2–3 son clave. Use that alignment
for the illustrated phrase. The recording and Sher score still need a
full form map. [1929 edition and MIDI](../repertoire/ma-teodora-cuba-musical-1929.md)
· [FIU disc record](https://latinpop.fiu.edu/albumdd.cfm?bid=7235&term=Como+traigo+la+yuca)
· [Trémolo, fig. 3](https://www.ucundinamarca.edu.co/documents/sedes/zipaquira/Tremolo_3.pdf)

**Conjunto and Tejano.** The Library of Congress National Recording Registry's
Narciso Martínez essay traces the accordion and Santiago Almeida's bajo sexto
and subsequent additions including drums and bass. Smithsonian Folkways'
Tony De La Rosa recording credits a live drummer and bass with accordion and
bajo sexto. The Library of Congress Houston project and Julián González
interview document a different urban Chicano network with conjunto influences.
Contemporary interviews with La Mafia describe accordion, synths and the
band's broadening repertoire, with drums credited in press and member
accounts. Houston's 1994 VIVA! report independently documents Rodney
Alejandro as Valentino's keyboardist. A derivative biography names an R-8 in
his setup, still a gear lead pending a first-person credit or archival item.
The Tejano cumbia study retains kit and bass, with optional machine conga.
The conjunto polka remains a backing ensemble with no 727 substitution.
Sources: [Recording Registry](https://memory.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/LaChicharronera_Saldana.pdf),
[Folkways track credits](https://folkways.si.edu/tony-de-la-rosa-y-su-conjunto/atotonilco/latin/music/track/smithsonian),
[Sonidos de Houston](https://www.loc.gov/collections/sonidos-de-houston-documenting-the-citys-chicano-music-scene/about-this-collection/),
[Julián González interview log](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/afc/afc2022009/afc2022009_006/afc2022009_006_ms001/afc2022009_006_ms001.pdf),
[La Mafia interview](https://www.dallasobserver.com/music/tejano-outsiders-define-whats-in-6402339/),
[Valentino 1994 coverage](https://www.vivamagazine.net/uploads/7/3/0/0/73006281/v6_n34.pdf),
[Valentino archive lead](https://docs.gato.txst.edu/747166/Hernandez.pdf).
The [San Antonio dance rehearsal](SAN-ANTONIO-DANCE-REHEARSAL.md) compares
the live drum and conga credits on the 1982 Los Polkeros cuts and pairs
them with three original Compás setups. The [Canales broadcast and Tejano
score dossier](SCENES-06-TEJANO-CANALES.md) pairs six published notation routes
with separate commercial recordings and documents three Selena show performances.

**A documented 727 crossover.** Jessica Swanston Baker’s University of Pennsylvania dissertation
drawing on Caribbean fieldwork reports Seventeen Plus pairing TR-707 and
TR-727 with live drums by 1987 in the Leeward Islands. Its wylers chronology
also documents Alesis and Yamaha riddim boxes and the hands-on work of
retrieving sounds at fêtes and carnival. Roland's DJ Pierre material and 727
history place the same palette in club music. These are independent contexts
for the same percussion vocabulary. The rack lets Compás play those voices
over a native beat or an already running ensemble.
Sources: [wylers fieldwork, pp. 34–36](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/214182156.pdf),
[DJ Pierre interview](https://articles.roland.com/lifetime-achievement-dj-pierre-and-phuture/),
[Roland TR-727 history](https://www.roland.com/ca/products/rc_tr-727/).

## The panel and the 727

Roland's chronology documents R-8 and R-8M, and the 1983 TR-909. I used the
TR-909 as the working interpretation of “R-9” because a period Roland model
with the latter name was not substantiated. The R-8's playable pads and
accent, nuance and Feel controls suggest that a struck voice should change
character with articulation. The TR-909 and 727 give Compás direct step
placement, visible pattern state and immediate instrument access. This build
uses named direct pads, a two-level live accent, a Feel knob, a dedicated fill
button, and an explicit queued section. It leaves roll, flam, real-time
overdub and the R-8's deep per-event edits for later design.
Sources: [Roland chronology](https://rolandcorp.com.au/blog/roland-drum-machine-chronicle-1964-2016),
[R-8M manual](https://cdn.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/R-8M_OM.pdf),
[TR-909 manual](https://cdn.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/TR-909_OM.pdf),
[TR-727 manual](https://cdn.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/TR-727_OM.pdf).

The rack implements all fifteen documented voices with the retained DMC
Orig 44.1 kHz recordings. Every Studio WAV is checked against its intake
hash. Compás keeps the existing trim and end fade, including the retained
quijada and star chime tails, applies Saccoia's measured normal-hit ratios
and packs the resulting PCM losslessly. The calibration concerns the
relative level of isolated hits. The original analog output stage, exact
accent law and repeat transients remain separate. The recordings are the
Compás author's own; see `LICENSING.md`. [Voice groups, reference and checks](LIVE-SET-AND-727.md#727-voice-allocation-and-balance)
