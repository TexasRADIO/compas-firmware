# A tribute to Johnny Canales

Johnny Canales brought Tejano, conjunto and other regional Mexican acts to a
television audience across the United States and Mexico. He started the show in
Corpus Christi in 1983. The Texas House tribute records his radio beginnings
at Radio Jalapeño, his support for Selena and other young performers, and his
sign-off, “You got it, take it away!” Compás borrows that spirit for a compact
rhythm section built to leave room for the singer, keyboardist and bandleader.
[Texas House resolution](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/83R/billtext/html/HR01494F.htm)
· [Congressional tribute](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/crec/170/e/795)

The new BB-Bob-style illustration uses the supplied Johnny Canales photograph
for the likeness and the existing BB-Bob character artwork for its ink and
color treatment. The FM-1 opens with the portrait and briefly brings Johnny back beside
the beat lamps when a different song loads. The Chromium bench gives him
a brief entrance on opening and at ensemble changes. The stage display
notes describe the timing and controls.

## Gear leads and evidence

| Gear | What the source establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Linn LM-1 and LinnDrum | Roger Linn's museum identifies two distinct sample-based machines. LinnDrum followed the LM-1 in 1982. [Roger Linn Design](https://www.rogerlinndesign.com/about/about-museum) |
| Roland TR-808 and TR-707 | The 808 makes analog drum sounds. The 707 uses PCM drum samples, including its own cowbell and handclap. [Roland's 707 and 727 history](https://articles.roland.com/mod-squad-a-history-of-tr-707-and-tr-727-modifications/) |
| Alesis SR-16 | Alesis dates its introduction to 1990, within the show's run. [Alesis company history](https://www.alesis.com/company) |
| Roland R-8 | Jorge Medina Martínez documents its use in Mexican solo keyboard performances. This is evidence for the workflow Compás adapts. [Roland México](https://articles.roland.com/the-legacy-of-the-r-8-human-rhythm-composer-in-mexico/) |

The supplied retailer listings identify products, and the Juno Reactor
“Pistolero” clip concerns a separate production. The references examined did
not verify a particular model used by a named Canales-show act in a dated
performance. The Texas State Ramón Hernández collection preserves material on
Canales and his show for follow-up. The existing scene dossier covers the
distinct musical settings behind Compás's rhythm studies.
