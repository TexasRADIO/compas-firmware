## Six reference titles

(QV-8050) identifies broadcasts from 1988–1994 and gives chapter numbers
for the three performances below. The 2011 Canales interview discusses
selecting material with Abraham Quintanilla. Use the chapter list to identify
each performance. Capitol/EMI's shorter 2010 *Selena: Performances* release
announced nine numbers and omits these three. The 1993 Corpus Christi
concert album and the 1995 Houston Astrodome concert are separate

## La Mafia in Houston and on Canales

*Me Estoy Enamorando* adds a Houston Tejano ballad to the notation
catalog. Musicnotes MN0117025 lists Armando Larrinaga, five pages for
piano, voice and guitar, B-flat major, 2/2 and a half-note pulse of
60. The score listing is separate from the Sony studio recording.
Sony credits Michael Aguilar on drums, Rudy Martinez on bass guitar,
Leonard Gonzales on guitar, Mando Lichtenberger on accordion and
keyboards, and David de la Garza on keyboards. The published tempo
corresponds to a quarter-note clock of 120. It gives a starting point
for playing from the chart. The recording's tempo, drum machine use
and phrase structure still need their own evidence. Sony supplies a
1992 phonogram notice and November 3, 1992 release metadata. Apple
shows a 1991 date alongside the 1992 notice. The catalog uses the
phonogram year and leaves the release-date discrepancy visible here.
[Exact score edition](https://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/la-mafia/me-estoy-enamorando/MN0117025)
· [Sony recording and credits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_PcBl_5kEFI)

The *San Antonio Express-News* identifies *Nuestra Canción* as a 1991
Canales performance. The band’s *Estás Tocando Fuego* album version
is a separate source. A later merengue by Elvis Crespo provides a
direct comparison of what changes when the song moves between
rhythm sections. Crespo recalls working from a La Mafia cassette and
receiving a sequence from arranger Marcos Carrera for his version.
That account documents Crespo’s rehearsal process. The published
credits for the *Suavemente* cut also list José Dario on drums and
Héctor Herrera on percussion. They leave the sequence's role on the
finished recording open. The digital credits name Ricardo Quijano
on a La Mafia issue and Homero D’Rodríguez on the Crespo release.
Keep those attributions with their versions pending a check of the
liner notes and publisher records. Both versions remain listening
references while an exact score and reuse rights are established.

## What the instrument should preserve

- *Ven Conmigo* documents the difference between a Tejano cumbia and a
  polkita on the same record. Joe Nick Patoski identifies Fito Olivares as an
  influence on *Baila Esta Cumbia*. For Compás, that supports keeping the
  cumbia’s kit and bass distinct from its conjunto polka. The Tejano cumbia
  study is original. [Library of Congress registry essay](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Ven-Conmigo_Patoski.pdf)
- The GRAMMY Museum documents Suzette Quintanilla’s snare and A.B.
  Quintanilla’s bass. The commercial *La Llamada* track credits separately
  list Suzette on drums, Pete Astudillo on percussion, and Ricky Vela on
  keyboards and programming. The credits leave the programming device
  and its signal path open. Compás keeps the optional machine percussion
  on separate switches from the band rhythm. [Museum exhibit](https://www.grammy.com/news/selena-from-texas-to-the-world-exhibit-grammy-museum/)
  · [Track credits](https://music.apple.com/us/song/714064735)
- Olivares described his Mexican tropical influences and his move to
  Houston in 1980. Tejano singer and archivist Veronique Medrano describes
  the saxophone as central to his cumbia phrasing. For a solo show, that
  points toward accompaniment with space for a horn or keyboard hook.
  [Olivares interview](https://www.chron.com/life/article/saxo-en-la-ciudad-olivares-se-va-al-centro-con-2111725.php)
  · [Medrano interview](https://texasstandard.org/stories/fito-olivares-death-cumbia-saxophonist-rey-juana-cubana/)
- Selena’s *Techno Cumbia* is a specific Tejano production. The Peruvian
  tecnocumbia study has its own sources and arrangement decisions.
  [Regional electronic cumbia notes](REGIONAL-RHYTHMS.md)

## Rehearse the rhythm section

Choose **Tejano rhythm section** in the offline hub. Raise MASTER and
press PLAY. Turn PRESETS to queue each mix on the next phrase. All four
slots use the same original two-bar Compás cumbia at 105 BPM, with the
same bass line until the last slot. The 727 supplies the conga voice.
The kick, snare, hat, güiro and bass use Compás synthesis. The fixed
groove makes it easy to hear what each layer contributes.

| Slot | Compás plays | Space for the musician |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kit and Bass | Kick, snare, hat and bass | Play a scraper or conga against the backbeat. |
| Full Rhythm Band | Kit, güiro, conga and bass | Play the hook or sing with the complete backing. |
| Hand Perc and Bass | Güiro, conga and bass | Play kit or compare the hand percussion against the same bass line. |
| Play Your Bass | Kit, güiro and conga | Play bass or cover the bass line on keys. |

The study follows the division of roles audible and credited in this
music. Its notes, tempo and part choices are Compás arrangements.
