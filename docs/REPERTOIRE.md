# Compás repertoire intake

The catalog prioritizes
source material a player can use to rehearse an actual arrangement, with
Tejano cumbia, Colombian cumbia, Cuban son and bolero, salsa and Dominican
merengue as distinct jobs for the rhythm section. Menudo and Flans extend the
research into 1980s Spanish-language pop. The 1992 Selena single belongs to the
next chapter of the Johnny Canales era.

The offline Set Builder has a searchable Repertoire Sources drawer. Search by
title, credit or style. It links to the exact editions and embeds the piano
backing and canto guide as separate MIDI downloads. Each playable edition
also has a type-1 rehearsal MIDI with the parts on separate tracks. The set slots accept
La Paloma, La Golondrina, El Manisero, Cielito Lindo, La Cucaracha, La Adelita, La Mora, Juana Quilina, Zacatecas March, Siboney, Má Teodora, Sobre las olas, Júrame and La Valentina in twenty-eight registered arrangements,
alongside the existing Compás grooves. The playable edition cards let you select the exact arrangement and append it
to a free slot or explicitly replace a numbered slot. Export the running
order to play it on the browser bench or FM-1 emulator. Melody-only downloads
remain separate from playable backing arrangements.

### Border song editions

For *Allá en el Rancho Grande*, the Morgan catalogs a Salvador Cabrera
voice-and-piano print under Silvano R. Ramos, c1926, with a dedication on
p. 2 and a Mexico City shop stamp on the copy. Its record is 159383. The
U.S. Copyright Office entry is 15800 on printed p. 828, vol. 22, part 3.
It names Ramos, a revision by J. López Alavez, two voices, a March 10,
1927 copyright date and an April 30 deposit. The printed number is
**E 663020**. The archive OCR gives 668020. The 1927 Wagner y Levien
item in the Hote Casella inventory instead credits Emilio D. Uranga's
arrangement and J. D. del Moral's words. Keep all three records separate
until their notes, instrumentation and imprints can be compared.

### La Valentina in Texas

### La Adelita, corrido and San Antonio polka

Kemp’s *Adelita* (2016), IMSLP 666981, is a four-recorder arrangement of
the traditional corrido. The complete MIDI in Set Builder transcribes
all 73 written 2/4 measures on separate tracks for both altos, tenor and
bass. It uses the score’s approximate quarter-note tempo of 100 and keeps
the printed pitch, 32 ties and the final written rest. The first bars of
each printed page serve as MIDI locators. Playback uses General MIDI
recorder and fixed velocity. The attribution and CC BY 4.0 link travel
with both the complete score and the nine-bar opening study.

The transcription was checked against the staff positions, barlines,
stems and beams in the four-page PDF. Each of the 292 written staff bars
fills 48 ticks at 24 PPQ, and the four staves agree at shared note onsets.
The 791 written noteheads yield 759 attacks after resolving ties. The
sustained alto entrances across systems at bars 29 and 57, the tenor ties
at bars 68–69 and 71–72, and the final held alto notes were checked
against the printed systems. The quartet is available in the score picker
and as a separate MIDI download in Set Builder.
[Score and licence](https://imslp.org/wiki/La_Adelita_(Folk_Songs,_Mexican))
· [Full transcription ledger](../repertoire/la-adelita-kemp-quartet.json)

Open **Compas-La-Adelita.html**, enable audio, raise MASTER and press
PLAY. The first slot has Alto 1 on the Guide key. Switch it off to play
that part over Alto 2, tenor and bass. The other slots give you the
existing Conjunto Polka study and the same quartet with Guide off.
PRESETS cues each slot on the current bar or phrase. The score uses
the Compás synth at printed pitch, with no automatic percussion.
Intro, Verse, Chorus and Coda locate written bars 1, 5, 25 and 71.
Those cue names and locations are Compás rehearsal choices. The last
one brings in the three written closing bars.

### Siboney, Feist print and recorded bolero

Ernesto Lecuona’s *Siboney* appears in the New York Leo. Feist edition
as *Danzon Cubano*. York University’s John Arpin copy, JAC006835,
credits Dolly Morse for the American lyric and shows a 1929 copyright
notice and plate 6212-4. Compás uses the notes of that U.S. edition.
The export contains the written pitches, durations and form markers.
It carries no lyrics or archive images.
[York catalog](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/d2e6d3e5-9138-4466-b6c0-f8c964a79dc7) ·
[U.S. Copyright Office term guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf)

The printed chorus changes from the preceding three-flat signature to
C major. The playable excerpt takes the pickup and first two phrases
from page 4, systems 1–3. It includes 15 full 2/4 bars between two
one-beat cuts and ends at the long second cadence. The MIDI holds the
printed ties, retains the piano triplets and separates the lower staff,
upper staff, counterline and vocal guide. Two sung grace notes take
one tick each from the following note. Tempo and fixed velocities are
rehearsal settings. The piano part includes the doubled vocal figures
written in the score.
[Transcription ledger](../repertoire/siboney-feist-chorus.json)

The longer rehearsal follows the pickup, 31 common bars and the nine bars
marked **Last** on page 5. It goes directly to that ending and restores the
printed three-flat key signature. The verses, first and second endings,
and repeated D.S. route are outside this single-pass arrangement. At the
ad lib. passage the guide follows the lower printed vocal line. The MIDI
has separate tracks for both piano counterlines and the primary vocal guide.
Fermatas use their written durations at a steady 100 BPM for rehearsal.
The two rolled bass chords play together, with fixed velocities and
written note lengths throughout.
[Last ending transcription ledger](../repertoire/siboney-feist-last.json)

Open **Compas-Siboney.html** or choose **Siboney** in the rehearsal
picker. The first black key toggles the guide. The first two slots are
the opening study. Slots 3 and 4 play the longer form as **Siboney Last
Guide** and **Siboney Last Piano**. PRESETS changes slots at a written
barline during playback. The longer form’s section keys cue the pickup,
first phrase, third phrase and final cadence. Both versions are available
as score slots and MIDI in Set Builder.

Slots 5 and 6 add the **first-verse arrangement**. They play the piano
introduction, the two written bars after the segno twice, the first
sung verse and one chorus through the printed **Last** ending. Both
piano staves run through all 82 written and repeated bars. The guide
and the separate treble and bass counterlines each have their own MIDI
track. The first sung entrance has two eighth notes, C and E-flat, in
the lead. The verse closes with a held low C and a half bar before
the chorus's E-natural and G pickup. There is no automatic percussion.

Use **SIBONEY VERSE** to learn the melody and **SIBONEY VERSE PNO** to
sing over the piano. The four cue keys take you to the piano pickup,
