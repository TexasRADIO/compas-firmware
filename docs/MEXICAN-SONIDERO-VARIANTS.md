# Mexican sonidero: performance ecology, repertoire lanes and pattern candidates

20 November 2026 · Development research for the next Compás pattern bank.

“Sonidero” is not one drum pattern. It identifies a social and technical
practice of selection, mixing, amplification, announcement and dancing. A
useful machine therefore needs separate things: playable arrangements, live
performance controls, and an honest indication of what it cannot turn into a
single loop.

## What Compás already separates

| Lane | Present Compás material | What it represents | Do not collapse it into |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CDMX Sonidera | `cdmx` | An authored cumbia accompaniment with space for a saludo | A canonical Mexico City beat |
| Monterrey / rebajada | `mty-dub` plus REBAJADA | A separate authored slow-dub accompaniment, and a speed/pitch performance control | A fixed slow genre tempo or a CDMX pattern |
| Puebla synth cumbia | `puebla` | An authored keyboard-and-timbal accompaniment | All Poblano sonidero practice |
| San Luis Potosí Wepa | `wepa` | An authored accompaniment with live-saludo space | Puebla, CDMX, or tribal guarachero |
| Tribal guarachero | `tribal` | An independent 132 BPM club study | A sonidero sub-beat |

This distinction reflects the existing source ledger. Lippman’s fieldwork
describes a sonidero’s selection, dedications and hand-ducked speech over a
record, while the CDMX municipal and Fonoteca records document multiple
neighbourhood and operator histories. Those are performance and circulation
facts; they do not yield a universal sixteen-step grid. The existing CDMX
study remains an authored accompaniment and the greeting remains a live action,
not an automatic sampled slogan.

## The family has four layers

The useful discovery is not a longer list of names for the same loop. The
official CDMX declaration describes a practice made from record collections,
lighting and sound, locution, greetings, dance clubs, organisers, graphic
work and technical invention. It separately lists cumbia, salsa, guaracha,
vallenato, chicha and son montuno as repertoires circulated at the dances.
The layers below keep those facts from being reduced to one percussion preset.

| Layer | What it contains | Compás consequence | What it cannot license |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dance / sound-system ecology** | Operator, record selection, amplification, lighting, dance, salutations and mixing | A clear live cue, optional player-authored saludo, a level-dip/echo performance route and set sequencing | A canned greeting, a generic city loop or a claim to reproduce a named operator |
| **Repertoire family** | Cumbia, salsa, guaracha, vallenato, chicha and son montuno in the CDMX declaration | Keep the existing individually labelled rhythm families selectable; add only source-labelled cells from each family | Treat every one as a cumbia ornament or call the list a new Mexican genre |
| **Regional scene / production practice** | CDMX, Puebla, Monterrey, San Luis Potosí and distinct club/electronic routes | Preserve separate authored studies, city names and source scopes | Make one city stand for another, or make a regional name promise a transcription |
| **Performance transform** | Slow-pitch playback practices, a live voice insertion, dub space or a cue into a record change | Keep REBAJADA and future live controls outside the pattern editor’s genre labels | Freeze a transform into a falsely canonical drum beat |

This is also why the machine needs two kinds of browse action: **RHYTHM** for
an accompaniment/role map and **SET** for repertoire order. A sonidero set can
move from a cumbia to salsa or guaracha without claiming that those have the
same timekeeping logic. The official declaration’s list is a repertoire map,
not a factory preset list.

## New research map

| Candidate | What is established | Machine proposal | Status before a new pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CDMX / Peñón–Tepito–Neza dance practice** | Sonidero culture includes record selection, speech/dedications, mixer ducking and effects; CDMX, Neza and individual sounds have distinct histories | Improve the live *saludo* / duck / echo workflow and keep several record-derived rhythm families selectable | No new generic rhythm: requires a named record or performer role map |
| **Puebla cumbia sonidera / cumbia poblana** | Puebla has a locally named sonidero movement and groups; public and participant accounts distinguish it from CDMX repertoire | Audit `puebla`; create an additional *Puebla band* study only after a local musician identifies a repeatable accompaniment role distinction | Source and practitioner comparison needed; do not equate “synth” with the whole city |
| **Monterrey colombias and rebajada** | Rebajada is a playback/speed practice, not one rhythm; the Colombian-record repertoire and local dance culture are central | Keep REBAJADA as a performance transform; build record-family comparison studies only from named, lawful, role-level evidence | No new “rebajada beat” |
| **Wepa, San Luis Potosí** | Potosí research ties the term to deep voice, named barrios and greetings over a cumbia-like rhythm; it is related to but not synonymous with *sonidero* | Keep `wepa` as an authored study with the saludo live; deepen it through a Potosí operator/musician role map | Current loop remains an authored study, not a Kiss Sound imitation |
| **Guaracha in sonidero repertoires** | Puebla legislative and scene material lists guaracha alongside cumbia, salsa, son and other dance repertories | Treat as a separate repertoire family with its own rhythm sources, never as an ornament inside cumbia | Needs a primary rhythm source and musician check |
| **Rock sonidero, Neza/Chimalhuacán** | The Fonoteca’s current research programme identifies a local history but also says systematic research is scarce | Keep as a research lead for set architecture and sound/MC practice, not a pattern claim | Evidence discovery only |
| **Cumbia andina mexicana** | It is a live name in some public accounts, but the supplied sources do not yet establish a precise local pulse, instrumentation or lineage for Compás | Do not create a preset under this heading yet | Require a named musician/record and a specific role map |
| **Mixteca Baja tecladistas / *yaa sii*** | INAH fieldwork documents a keyboard-led local practice, cumbia influence in the bass and sonidero greetings; INAH and ECMO sources describe the dactylic organisation in 12/8 and/or 6/8, and warn that *yaa sii* is not simply the coastal chilena under another name | Keep it as a separate compound-meter product lead, not a 4/4 cumbia skin | Requires compound editor/target qualification plus a practitioner-approved role map |
| **Cumbia del sureste / “chunchaca”** | Mexican academic and university sources identify a sax/horn, key/guitar, acoustic-kit, tumbadora and flexible-bass ensemble route, distinct from CDMX tropical and other regional names | `cumbia-sureste`: an explicitly authored role-based development study; human lead remains unprogrammed | Southeast musician review of balance, tempo range and terminology |

## What the wider Mexico map changes

The closer finding is that “regional” does not always mean “a new beat.” INAH
describes an active central circuit extending from CDMX through Estado de
México, Morelos, Puebla, Tlaxcala and Hidalgo. That establishes communities,
routes and a listening public; it does **not** establish six discrete
percussion grammars. Calling each one a factory rhythm would manufacture
difference where the evidence currently shows a connected practice.

The next Compás pass should therefore distinguish four kinds of discovery:

| Discovery | Evidence so far | Correct Compás response | Not yet justified |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Central circuit** — CDMX/Edomex/Morelos/Puebla/Tlaxcala/Hidalgo | Institutional work maps a connected sonidero geography, and studies of dedications show that circulation and voice travel with it | A central-circuit *set* and better live operator moves: a visible cue, manual saludo space, duck/echo return and record-to-record handoff | A state-by-state “authentic beat” pack |
| **Tropical routing** | CDMX’s declaration and UNAM research enumerate cumbia, salsa, guaracha, vallenato, charanga, son, porro, rumba and more as repertoires in sound-system programming | Browse as distinct rhythm families; make an honest running order able to move among them | One composite “sonidero” 16-step grid |
| **Hybrid digital/live cumbia** | Radio Educación documents Digital Charanga combining prepared digital tracks and live musicians | Treat Compás as an accompaniment machine: leave a lead/response role open, carry parts across cues, and make fills player-triggered | A fake full-band recording or a named style without musician review |
| **Cumbia editada, León** | A scene report identifies computer edits layered over older songs | Retain as a research lead for a future edit/arrangement tool | An `EDITADA` preset or a regional claim based on one report |

This gives the instrument a more useful family tree. The **rhythm bank** holds
evidence-bounded accompaniments; the **set browser** holds programme choices;
and **live transforms** hold rate/pitch, duck, echo and player-triggered
transitions. The three must never be silently swapped for one another.

### Candidate central-circuit review set — architecture only

No new city loop should be added at this stage. For musician review, the
existing machine can instead audition the family as an intentionally varied
programme:

1. CDMX Sonidera — authored accompaniment with a speech/lead gap.
2. Puebla Sintes — authored keyboard-and-timbal study, explicitly narrow in
   scope.
3. Salsa Son — repertoire family, not CDMX cumbia.
4. Guaracha (once its existing source cell is separately surfaced) — own
   family, not a cumbia fill.
5. Monterrey Cumbia Dub — authored crossover, then apply **REBAJADA** as a
   player-controlled transform rather than hiding it in the title.
6. Potosí Wepa — an authored accompaniment with the documented voice/saludo layer left live.
7. Cumbia del Sureste — a role study with lead space left to the player.

The programme makes the family audible without pretending that Morelos,
Tlaxcala or Hidalgo are unexplained variations of a kick pattern. Any future
local slot needs either a named-source rhythm cell or a practitioner-approved
role map.

### Puebla is a family inside the family

The Puebla dossier is more precise than a generic “Puebla synth cumbia” label.
It reports how people in the local sonidero world sort material while dancing:

| Local listening category | What the dossier establishes | Compás response | What must not happen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Poblanas** | Slow, lyrical pieces with relatively little variation for complex dance sequences | Retain a slower, singer/lead-friendly accompaniment option; the current `puebla` study may be auditioned in that neighbourhood of use, but is not renamed as a canonical Poblana | Claim `puebla` reproduces all *poblanas*, or derive a grid from the verbal description |
| **Peruanas** | Andean-rooted repertory with fast, constant tempo | Keep it visibly a Peruvian/Andean repertoire inquiry, not a Mexican regional preset; this is a source-acquisition and musician-review task | Fold Peruvian music into a generic “Puebla” kit or claim Mexican authorship |
| **Peñoneras** | Repeated changes in rhythm and speed that enable more dance adornment | A future **player-controlled** phrase-change / tempo-transition mode is more faithful than a static `PEÑONERA` pattern | Automate tempo lurches invisibly, or treat a Peñón dance label as a fixed drum loop |

This is a particularly important design correction: the real regional
distinction can be **how an operator stages change for dancers**, not merely
which sound occupies step 13. Compás already has visible part cueing; a later
Peñonera-oriented performance proposal should build on that, with the player
arming any pace change and the display counting it in. It is deliberately not
implemented in this pattern-bank candidate.

## Regional distinctions worth preserving

The next bank should use the following taxonomy. “Implemented” means a
Compás-authored study or control already exists; it does **not** mean the
machine has authenticated a definitive regional grid. “Research only” means
the name is retained so it is not silently folded into generic cumbia.

| Lane | Kind | Current Compás handling | Evidence-bound next move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CDMX sonidera** | Sound-system ecology plus authored accompaniment | `cdmx`: scraper/conga/bell space, live saludo gap | A named-record cell or a practitioner-reviewed role map, never “the CDMX beat” |
| **CDMX electronic sonidera** | Producer / live-rig practice | Retain the CDMX study; its role source is conga, bell, güiro/guacharaca, voice and live delay | A separate digital study only after documenting a particular arrangement’s role separation |
| **Puebla cumbia sonidera** | Local dance and band/keyboard practice | `puebla`: labelled authored synth-cumbia study | Compare a Puebla player’s role map with a CDMX map before creating a second Puebla lane |
| **Monterrey colombias** | Record repertoire and local dance history | `mty-dub`: clearly labelled crossover study | Build only a named-record family comparison; it must not be called rebajada |
| **Rebajada** | Playback-rate / pitch transform, documented in more than one Mexican context | REBAJADA control; `mty-dub` stays separate | Preserve rate + pitch + tail coupling; cite the operator/context for any extreme setting |
| **San Luis Potosí wepa** | A Potosí sonidero sound-world shaped by deep voice, named barrios and greetings over cumbia-like rhythm | `wepa`: labelled authored study; programmed cuts are explicitly Compás authorship | Obtain local operator/musician confirmation of repeatable rhythm and pace roles before expanding it |
| **Tribal guarachero** | Distinct club/dance scene with Mexican regional routes | `tribal`: separate authored 132 BPM study | Keep it out of the sonidero sub-beat menu; new cells need their own source chain |
| **Salsa / guaracha / son montuno** | Repertoire families in sonidero programming | Existing individually named Compás families | Grow from their own source-cell ledgers, not from the CDMX study |
| **Vallenato / chicha** | Repertoire families and record routes | No local sonidero-labelled pattern is asserted | Research source labels, instrumentation and performer practice before adding anything |
| **Rock sonidero** | Neza/Chimalhuacán lead | Research only | First build an oral-history/source dossier; no beat is implied |
| **Mexican cumbia andina** | Unresolved public label | Research only | Establish a named performer/record, locale and stable role map before a lane exists |
| **Mixteca Baja tecladistas / *yaa sii*** | Keyboard-led local practice with cumbia influence and a documented saludo route | Research only: its documented 6/8 or 12/8 form is outside the present engine | Add compound meter first; do not press it into 4/4 |
| **Cumbia del sureste** | South/southeast ensemble route, distinct from the names used for CDMX and Andean practices | `cumbia-sureste`: authored role-based development study | Southeast musician review; it is not a transcription or an all-region claim |

## Playable family audition — not a claim of equivalence

[`mexican-sonidero-family-set.json`](../repertoire/mexican-sonidero-family-set.json)
is an eight-slot listening order for the current machine. It deliberately
alternates authored local studies with independently named repertoire or
adjacent-scene studies so the player can hear the distinction:

1. CDMX Sonidera — authored accompaniment, live-saludo space.
2. Puebla Sintes — authored Puebla keyboard/timbal study.
3. Monterrey Cumbia Dub — authored crossover study, **not** rebajada.
4. Salsa Son — an existing salsa family; no CDMX provenance is asserted.
5. Son Montuno — an existing separate family; no CDMX provenance is asserted.
6. Potosí Wepa — authored accompaniment; the saludo remains a player action.
7. Cumbia Sureste — authored role-based study, with the horn lead left live.
8. Tribal 132 — adjacent Mexican club study, explicitly not a sonidero
   sub-beat.

Load it through **Open set or show**. Use RHYTHM/Presets to cue entries at a
phrase boundary. REBAJADA remains a live transform the player may audition on
an authored backing; it is intentionally not hidden in the title of a slot.
The order is a review tool, not a historic sonidero running order and not a
released factory set.

## Build rule

Every new Mexican lane must declare whether it is:

1. a **performance control** (for example, rebajada or a live duck),
2. a **repertoire selector** (for example, a source-labelled guaracha cell),
3. an **authored accompaniment study**, or
4. a **source-cell reduction**.

No new entry is titled merely “Mexican Sonidera.” Its city, practice, source
scope and authorial contribution must be visible in the ledger and on the
review set. This is what keeps the instrument broad without pretending that
one machine arrangement contains the scene.
