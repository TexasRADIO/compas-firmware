Private development, 21 November 2026. This separate branch adds one playable
study to the measured 36-study bank. It does not replace the RC3 install package.

## What the reference actually supports

Diana Laura Galeana Leyva's *Resonancias guerrerenses* (UNAM Facultad de Música,
2025), printed p. 19 / PDF p. 23, Figure 8, reproduces bars 102–103 of
María Carpio's four-hand-piano arrangement of Álvaro Carrillo's *Alingo Lingo*.
The prose identifies a 6/8 accompaniment representing guitar/harp functions.
The retained full page and enlarged excerpt were read visually. This is one
reader's rhythmic reduction of this named chamber arrangement, pending a second
notation reading and Costa Chica musician comparison.

## What Compás authors

The source provides the adopted onset relationships only. All pitches,
velocities, note lengths, relative levels, percussion support, fills and live
form are authored. The single-note pluck gives a chord-attack cue; it does
not reproduce the source piano voicing or emulate a harp/guitar. The source
excerpt has no metronome mark. **96 dotted-quarter BPM** is a rehearsal choice;
it produces a 2.5-second two-bar phrase and quarter-note MIDI clock at 144 BPM.

The initial mix is Strum 68, Bass 76, quiet Eighths 24 and Hand reply 30.
Low cue and Rim start at zero. Low cue doubles the bass rhythm if wanted;
the optional percussion is an arrangement choice, not an extracted coastal
drum pattern. No 727 kit is assigned. The live lead is left to the player.
The four black-key layers use separately authored 6/8-compatible cells; they
start off. Their conga/maraca/timbal/bell names identify the machine sounds,
not a traditional percussion instrumentation claim. See the meter guide for
their exact steps. The same compound support remains safe through step 48.

The six existing conductor keys remain Intro, Verse A, Verse B, Chorus, Break,
Ending. Intro exposes Strum/Bass before opening the ensemble in its second
bar; Verse B adds accents in bar two; Chorus lifts the source rhythm;
Break and Ending expose the two source roles. ARP adds a newly authored
last-beat reply into the selected part. This is a playable rehearsal form,
not the full *Alingo Lingo* arrangement.

## Play and review

Build with the real local bank, then open **Compas-Chilena-Review.html**.
PLAY starts; the six bright centre white keys cue the live form. SELECT keeps
its normal page function. The role editor has two pages, with only eight
valid steps on page two. The historic 727 editor refuses the 24-step role
grid explicitly. A 4/4 percussion preset is not stretched over this study.

First listen to Strum and Bass alone. Does the first bar's quarter-note
crossing and second bar's delayed bass attack read clearly? Then bring in
the authored support if useful. Compare the enlarged source excerpt before
commenting on onset accuracy. A regional player should judge whether this
reduction helps an accompanist and what its articulation still lacks.

This entry represents neither all Costa Chica chilenas nor Mixteca *yaa sii*.
The latter remains a distinct research lane; its 12/8 descriptions do not
provide an adopted programmable accompaniment yet. No public regional
authenticity or musician approval is claimed.
