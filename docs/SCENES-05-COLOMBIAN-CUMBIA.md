# Three Colombian Caribbean cumbia settings

The Colombian Ministry of Cultures safeguarding plan documents cumbia as
regional practice. For El Banco it describes a caña de millo ensemble with
tambor alegre, llamador, tambora and maracas and/or guache. The three Compás
studies preserve distinct traditional percussion cells. They leave melody,
harmony and bass to the player.

| Compás preset | Source | What to listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cumbia Banqueña · 94 | Ligio José Arce Martínez, Magdalena Sur, [PDF p. 75](https://mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Documents/plan-especial-de-salvagurdia-cumbia.pdf#page=75) | The alegre skips the second offbeat and answers on the fourth. |
| Barranquilla · 97 | Gusti De La Hoz Ferreira, Atlántico, [PDF p. 146](https://mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Documents/plan-especial-de-salvagurdia-cumbia.pdf#page=146) | The same llamador and tambora structure as Banqueña, with two additional alegre onsets per printed bar. |
| Cumbia Soledad · 110 | Gusti De La Hoz Ferreira, Atlántico, [PDF p. 147](https://mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Documents/plan-especial-de-salvagurdia-cumbia.pdf#page=147) | Llamador pickups lead into both offbeats. Tambora rim figures change between odd and even bars. |

## Play the comparison

Choose **Colombian cumbia** from the offline start page, or open the
[prepared set](../repertoire/colombian-cumbia-audition-set.json) from the
browser bench or Set Builder. PRESETS changes to the next study at the
end of the phrase. On 727 pads, these are the last three complete rhythms.
The keyboard still gives access to the conductor and optional overlays.
Mix gives independent levels and mute for all six percussion lanes.
Break isolates the llamador and guache. Base and Lift keep the documented
cell, with no added fills. Switch to Cumbia Costa for Compás's separate
original coastal arrangement.

## What the engine plays

Each figure is printed in cut time, with a one-quarter-note pickup and
four body bars. The four body bars form a loop. The body occupies two
displayed 4/4 bars in Compás. A printed half note is one Compás beat, so
printed eighth notes land on the Compás sixteenth grid and the printed
94, 97 and 110 marks retain their pulse. The anacrusis is outside the loop.

Alegre round versus cross heads and the longer llamador strokes receive
a fixed accent contrast. Tambora head and rim use separate synthesized
voices. Guache ties suppress new attacks and the synthesized scrape
decays within the held note. An initial strike restarts the phrase.
The written hand alternation and detailed stroke timbres remain part of
the reference. The stock kit uses Compás synthesis for all six roles.
Maraca and guache play together as printed, and can be separated in Mix.

The studies use documented traditional rhythm practice and original
Compás sound design. The source engravings, named works and recordings
remain reference material. These are accompaniment loops for comparing
three source examples. Listening review with a player is open.

## Edit the rhythms on the 727

For a live comparison, choose **Colombian cumbia on the 727** from the start
page or the stopped Rehearsal sets menu. PLAY starts Banqueña at 94 BPM.
PRESETS cues Barranquilla at 97 and Soledad at 110. The pattern and tempo
change together at the end of the phrase. Each slot opens in **Drums Only**
with its pattern ready to edit under REC → SEQ. **Save show** captures a
personal copy, including edits saved to the other slots.

**Download the 727 show** saves the same three-slot `.cpshow`. On Apple
Silicon, open it with the native show launcher paired to the current XIP and
WASM. The clean build emits `build/Colombian-Cumbia-727.cpshow`. It compiles
the show through the actual board controls and checks all three slots after
reopening it. The strict native run recalled all three attachments at
their intended tempos and confirmed a silent STOP. The XIP matches the
196-second load test below.
[Browser and native show checks](evidence/colombian-727-show-20261106.json)

On any 4/4 groove, press REC then SEQ and turn PRESETS to **BANQUEÑA 727**,
**BARRANQUILLA 727** or **SOLEDAD 727**. These are
positions 20–22 in the step editor. Set FX to **Drums Only** to hear the
rack on its own. Choose the matching song slot first to use its starting
tempo and straight Feel. A pattern change while playing lands on the
next bar and preserves the running two-bar position.

| Written role | 727 lane |
| --- | --- |
| Alegre, cross heads | Muted high conga |
| Alegre, round heads | Accented open high conga |
| Llamador | Low bongó, accented on the two main offbeats |
| Tambora rim and head | High timbale and low conga |
| Maraca | Maracas |
| Guache attacks | Quijada |

The rack uses one-shots. The quijada gives the guache an independent lane
so its attacks can coincide with the maracas. Cabasa and maracas share a
choke group. The guache's printed ties suppress retriggers, and the rack
plays its own decay. Choose the complete groove for the synthesized
guache and separate hand-drum synthesis. The round/cross contrast and the
llamador accents follow the same interpretation as that groove.
[Source figures, pp. 75, 146 and 147](https://mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Documents/plan-especial-de-salvagurdia-cumbia.pdf#page=75)

Use KNOB1 to choose the lane, OCT−/+ to choose the bar and the first sixteen
keys to edit. Save 727 steps exports the editable grid. GLO → SAVE attaches
it to a set slot. The three source identifiers follow Roland Step Write
in the saved-show format. They require this build when retained in a
`.cpshow`. A `.c7r` file carries the editable steps without the source
identifier. Source engravings and recordings stay in the reference ledger.

## Named repertoire

**Cumbia de Barranquilla** by Emilio Murillo is cataloged as two pages for
voice and piano, with an estimated manuscript date of 1900–1920. The asset
was inaccessible. The education catalog supplies a CC BY-NC-SA label, with
the exact scan and licence still to be checked. The Murillo song and Gusti
De La Hoz Ferreira’s percussion chart have separate entries.
[National library record via BDPI](https://www.iberoamericadigital.net/BDPI/Search.do?numfields=1&field1=control&field1val=005-165058&field1Op=AND&text=&advanced=true) ·
[Colombia Aprende record](https://redaprende.colombiaaprende.edu.co/metadatos/recurso/cumbia-de-barranquilla-partitura-general/)

**Cumbia Cienaguera** has a verified melody source. PDF page 36 prints
Carlos A. Guillot Monsalvo’s transcription of the early Andrés Paz Barros
instrumental melody, in 2/4 at quarter note = 120. Page 37 describes the
1951 accordion recording and credits a later lyric. Guillot’s arranged
excerpt on page 38 is a separate version. The melody is cataloged for
reference while playable-use permissions remain open.
[Safeguarding plan, PDF p. 36](https://mincultura.gov.co/direcciones/patrimonio-y-memoria/Documents/plan-especial-de-salvagurdia-cumbia.pdf#page=36)
